How to read this globe

Scope. This is a broad curated inventory of early, influential archaeological finds across inhabited continents, ending at 1000 BCE. It is not a complete global excavation database. Coverage and published counts are uneven.

Artifact colors. Marker colors follow a fixed logarithmic scale from 1 to 10,000+ reported items. These are source-defined samples: individual objects, lithic debris, fragments, ornaments, or wall images. A panel can be one unit; a hundred fragments need not be a hundred original objects. Lower bounds and approximate values are identified in each count description. Unknown counts are gray, never invented or treated as zero.

Not a population heat map. This is a count-colored point map with halos, not a continuous statistical density surface. Excavation effort, preservation, counting units, and publication history strongly affect the colors. Sites with one highlighted object may contain many other uncounted finds. No grand artifact total is reported because those units are not comparable.

Dates and the slider. The default shows the dated evidence only once its youngest stated date is reached. Colors describe the published sample, which may include material younger than the oldest dated evidence (see Bizmoune). “Possible earlier range overlap” instead shows it from the oldest boundary, with a dashed marker until the younger boundary is reached. This does not distribute a multi-period count across time or imply a continuous occupation. Minimum-age paintings appear at their minimum bound even though they could be older. The catalogue always lists the complete age range.

Time conventions. Cal BP is calendar years before 1950. A BP age is converted to approximate BCE by subtracting 1950. Deep geological ages are rounded estimates; the numeric slider is not evidence for one-year precision. Radiocarbon years are not calendar years; calibrated published ranges are used when available. The four timeline scales include linear recent-history views.

Bones rule. Skeletal material is included only as a documented directly radiocarbon-dated specimen or subset. Older fossils, skulls dated only by surrounding sediments, and unverified bone/ivory objects are excluded—even if famous. Thus no Lucy, dinosaur bones, Jebel Irhoud skulls, or unverified oracle-bone corpus is plotted as bones. Stone tools from their sites can still qualify. Shell/eggshell is identified separately from skeletal bone. Radiocarbon generally cannot date remains millions of years old; see Smithsonian dating guidance.

Locations. Latitude/longitude are approximate site or locality centers, not surveyed findspots; each detail card states its coordinate basis. Coordinates are for orientation, not field navigation. The globe uses modern coastlines—not reconstructed Ice Age shorelines or ancient borders. No present-day territorial claim is implied.

Sources and uncertainty. Research papers, institutional reports, museums and heritage authorities are preferred. Some entries use clearly linked secondary summaries. Claims described as “oldest” are tied to particular categories and research dates, not timeless rankings. A lack of mapped evidence is not evidence of absence.

Controls. Drag to rotate; wheel, pinch, or +/− to zoom. Arrow keys rotate the focused globe. Click a list entry to center it. Export downloads the entire researched catalogue, not just the currently filtered view. The site catalogue is searchable and sortable.
